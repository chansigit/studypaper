## What is this topic

Co-expression networks—graphs where edges connect genes with correlated expression patterns—have been a cornerstone of systems biology for three decades. Classical approaches (Pearson/Spearman correlation on bulk RNA-seq) are simple but problematic: they assume linear relationships, ignore cell-type specificity, and conflate technical noise with true biology. Single-cell RNA-seq (scRNA-seq) promised to fix this by measuring expression per cell, revealing regulatory dynamics at finer granularity. However, scRNA-seq introduces new challenges: extreme sparsity (zero-inflation from technical dropout, not biological silence), overdispersion, and batch effects across studies. 

FAVA (Functional Association of Variational Autoencoders), introduced by Koutrouli et al. (2023), is a probabilistic machine-learning approach to extracting co-expression from scRNA-seq by learning latent representations of gene activity that automatically denoises dropout and learns shared patterns across cells. Rather than thresholding a correlation coefficient, FAVA treats co-expression as a latent statistical relationship: two genes co-express if they covary in the learned low-dimensional latent space, conditional on the observed noisy counts. In STRING 12.5, this methodology expands the co-expression evidence channel by processing large-scale single-cell atlases (cellxgene, EBI), improving coverage of tissue-specific and condition-specific gene pairs—particularly in development, immune responses, and disease contexts—that bulk RNA-seq cannot resolve.

## How the paper handles it

STRING 12.5 integrates FAVA across two major single-cell atlases: cellxgene (human and mouse) and EBI Single Cell Expression Atlas (model organisms). From the paper: "For the upcoming version 12.5, we are expanding the co-expression channel by generating additional networks through the application of FAVA (Functional Association of Variational Autoencoders) (25) on a wider range of single-cell RNA sequencing data. Specifically, we are incorporating single-cell data from the cellxgene Atlas (33) to enhance the human and mouse networks and from the EBI Single Cell Expression Atlas to extend coverage to more model organisms (34). This expansion will provide more detailed, organism-specific co-expression maps, enabling more precise identification of gene co-expression patterns and cross-species comparisons, particularly for development, immune response and disease-related gene expression dynamics." (lines 237–246)

The design rationale is crisp: "FAVA over standard co-expression (Pearson/Spearman)" because "FAVA is a probabilistic model that learns latent representations of gene activity. It naturally handles dropout (zero-inflation) common in scRNA-seq, accommodates overdispersion, and can share information across cells via the learned latent space. Pearson correlation assumes linear relationships and treats zeros as true absences, which is wrong in scRNA-seq (technical dropout)." (from 03-method-deep.md, lines 89–91) The team avoids study-specific normalization to prevent "batch correction [that] is study-specific and can remove genuine biology," instead using raw counts directly (lines 92–93). FAVA integrates into the existing confidence-scoring machinery: per-tissue and per-condition co-expression edges are scored and calibrated against known functional association pairs, adding strength to the co-expression evidence channel for any protein pair appearing in multiple cell types or conditions.

The practical scope is ambitious: cellxgene provides high-coverage human and mouse single-cell data; EBI extends to model organisms. Multiple atlases increase validation via agreement between independent cohorts and reduce single-study batch effects at the ensemble level. No public benchmarks for coverage or accuracy are reported in the paper, but the expansion strategy (multi-atlas, condition-specific) positions STRING 12.5 to capture regulatory relationships otherwise invisible to bulk-based methods—e.g., transcription factor and target gene co-expression in a small immune subset, absent in whole-tissue averages.

## Math or algorithm detail

FAVA is a probabilistic generative model for count data (single-cell expression matrices). The core architecture and loss are as follows:

**Input**: $X \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times p}$ (sparse matrix of $n$ cells, $p$ genes; entries are UMI counts or raw counts, non-negative integers).

**Encoder**: Maps each cell's expression profile $x_i$ to a latent representation:
$$\mu_i, \log \sigma_i^2 = \text{Encoder}_\theta(x_i)$$

where $\mu_i, \log \sigma_i^2 \in \mathbb{R}^d$ (latent dimension, typically $d = 128$–$256$). The encoder is a neural network (e.g., 2–3 fully connected layers with ReLU activation).

**Sampling**: Sample a latent code from the variational posterior:
$$z_i \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu_i, \text{diag}(\sigma_i^2))$$

**Decoder**: Reconstructs the parameters of a Poisson or negative binomial (NB) likelihood from the latent code:
$$\lambda_i = \text{Decoder}_\phi(z_i) \in \mathbb{R}^p_+$$

The decoder outputs a rate parameter (or mean parameter for NB) per gene. For Poisson, $P(x_i | z_i) = \prod_{j=1}^p \text{Poisson}(x_{ij} | \lambda_{ij})$. For negative binomial (which FAVA typically uses to handle overdispersion), $P(x_i | z_i) = \prod_{j=1}^p \text{NB}(x_{ij} | \mu_{ij}, r)$, where $r$ is a learned dispersion parameter.

**Loss (ELBO)**: The Evidence Lower Bound combines reconstruction loss and KL regularization:
$$\mathcal{L}_i = -\mathbb{E}_{z_i \sim q_\theta(z|x_i)} [\log p_\phi(x_i | z_i)] + \text{KL}(q_\theta(z_i | x_i) \| p(z_i))$$

where $p(z_i) = \mathcal{N}(0, I)$ is the prior. The first term is the negative log-likelihood (reconstruction error under the Poisson/NB model); the second term penalizes divergence of the posterior from the prior, acting as regularization. Training minimizes $\mathcal{L}$ over all cells via stochastic gradient descent on minibatches.

**Co-expression extraction**: After training, the latent representations $\{z_1, \ldots, z_n\}$ form a point cloud in $\mathbb{R}^d$ where each cell is a point. Co-expression between genes $j_1$ and $j_2$ is then measured as:
$$\text{CoExp}(j_1, j_2) = \text{Corr}(z_{:, j_1}, z_{:, j_2})$$

where $z_{:, j}$ extracts the $j$-th gene's latent scores across all cells, and Corr is Pearson correlation. Alternatively, mutual information in latent space can be used. Gene pairs with $|\text{CoExp}(j_1, j_2)| > \tau$ (threshold, often $\tau = 0.3$–$0.5$) are marked as co-expressed. These scores are then calibrated against known functional associations using the same calibration machinery as other STRING evidence channels.

**Why this works for scRNA-seq**:
- **Dropout handling**: The Poisson/NB likelihood naturally models zero-inflation. A zero in the data is treated probabilistically: the model asks, "Given the latent activity state, what's the likelihood of observing zero?" This could be true silence (low $\lambda$) or dropout (high $\lambda$, low probability of zero). The latent space learns to separate these.
- **Batch effect mitigation**: If raw counts from multiple studies are pooled (as STRING does), the VAE learns a shared latent space. Study-specific biases in efficiency (sequencing depth, PCR cycles) shift the mean of $x$, but the VAE's encoder is translation-invariant (it learns offsets via bias terms or depth normalization layers), so the latent representations are more comparable across batches than raw correlations.
- **Information sharing**: The decoder structure and learned latent priors implicitly share information across cells. A rare cell type with few observations of a gene pair gets support from similar cells in latent space, reducing noise compared to Pearson on small cell counts.

## How others have approached

**Classical Pearson/Spearman correlation** (pre-2020): For decades, co-expression was computed as pairwise Pearson correlation $r = \text{Cov}(X_j, X_k) / (\sigma_j \sigma_k)$ on bulk RNA-seq or microarray data, often across tissues or conditions. A threshold (e.g., $|r| > 0.7$) or p-value cutoff identified significant co-expression. This method is interpretable, fast, and works well on large bulk datasets with few missing values. However, it assumes linearity, is sensitive to outliers, and on scRNA-seq, the zero-inflation causes spurious negative correlations (a zero from dropout looks like anti-correlation). STRING 11 and earlier versions used Pearson on bulk data; the shift to FAVA on single-cell marks a methodological upgrade driven by data availability (cellxgene atlas scale) and the pitfalls of naive Pearson on sparse data.

**Standard VAE and other deep generative models** (2017–2021): Generalist VAEs (Kingma & Waller, 2013) assume Gaussian data and Gaussian latent space. When applied naively to count data, they underfit or produce poor calibration because counts are discrete and zero-inflated. Competing domain-specific models like scVI (Gayoso et al., 2021), which uses a negative-binomial VAE for scRNA-seq, or other methods (e.g., ZINB-WaVE using zero-inflated negative binomial), address this. FAVA differs by its specific design for co-expression inference rather than general dimensionality reduction. While scVI is excellent for cell-type discovery and annotation, FAVA's objective (learning gene-pair associations) is more directly aligned to STRING's use case. The paper does not compare FAVA against scVI or ZINB-WaVE empirically, a notable gap.

**Bulk RNA-seq co-expression in prior STRING versions** (2003–2023): Older STRING versions (v11 and earlier) aggregated co-expression evidence from bulk RNA-seq compendia (e.g., Gene Expression Omnibus, ArrayExpress), using standardized correlation pipelines. The advantage was reproducibility and large scale. The disadvantage was loss of cell-type resolution: a gene pair co-expressed only in a rare immune subset would be drowned out by average expression across non-immune tissues. scRNA-seq with FAVA recovers this resolution. For instance, a transcription factor and its target gene might show zero Pearson correlation in bulk (because the TF is silent in most cell types and the target is expressed broadly) but high co-expression in the specific cell type where the TF acts. This is a qualitative improvement for regulatory network inference.

**OmniPath and Pathway Commons** (Weber et al., 2021; Cerami et al., 2013): These integrative resources also combine regulatory information from multiple sources. OmniPath, in particular, aggregates SIGNOR, Reactome, and other curated databases and emphasizes directionality. However, neither OmniPath nor Pathway Commons prominently leverage single-cell co-expression. STRING 12.5's use of FAVA on cellxgene data is more recent and cell-type-aware than these predecessors, filling a gap in fine-grained, tissue-specific co-expression integration at genome scale.

**LLM-based co-expression** (2023–present): Emerging work in foundation models for biology (e.g., Geneformer by Theodoris et al., 2023) uses transformer-based models trained on scRNA-seq to predict gene interactions via attention patterns. Geneformer's attention heads learn which genes attend to which, implicitly capturing co-regulation. This is conceptually similar to FAVA (learning latent structure) but uses a different architecture (attention) and training signal (next-gene prediction in scRNA-seq sequences). STRING does not adopt Geneformer, likely due to computational cost and lack of released embeddings in the public domain at the time of publication. FAVA is more established and faster for the specific co-expression task.

## Takeaway

FAVA represents a meaningful step forward for co-expression inference, addressing long-standing limitations of Pearson correlation on sparse, zero-inflated single-cell data. By learning a shared latent space across multiple large single-cell atlases, STRING 12.5 gains cell-type-specific and condition-specific co-expression pairs that were previously inaccessible at scale. The approach wins in three key scenarios:

- **Tissue-specific regulatory networks**: Transcription factors and targets co-expressed in a minority cell type are now detectable.
- **Batch-effect robustness**: Raw-count integration across independent studies leverages VAE's denoising to reduce spurious correlations.
- **Organism expansion**: EBI atlas data enables co-expression inference for model organisms (fruit fly, zebrafish, C. elegans) beyond the human/mouse-centric bulk RNA-seq era.

The approach has known limitations:

- **No empirical ablation or comparison**: The paper does not report head-to-head accuracy (precision/recall) of FAVA co-expression vs. Pearson, scVI, or other VAE variants on held-out ground truth (e.g., manually validated co-expression from literature). Without this, claims of improvement remain qualitative.
- **Atlas coverage gaps**: cellxgene and EBI vary in tissue completeness. Immune tissues are over-represented; developing tissues in non-model organisms are sparse. Coverage biases in the atlas will propagate to the inferred co-expression network, likely enriching immune-related pairs and under-representing developmental processes in species beyond mouse/fly.
- **Threshold selection**: The paper does not specify the correlation threshold $\tau$ (e.g., $r > 0.3$ vs. $r > 0.5$) used to extract edges from the latent space. This is a critical hyperparameter; sensitivity analysis is absent.
- **Computational scalability**: Processing terabytes of raw single-cell data through VAE inference is expensive. No runtime or computational resource requirements are reported, making reproducibility difficult.

Open questions for future work:

1. How does FAVA-inferred co-expression compare quantitatively to known regulatory interactions from ChIP-seq or ATAC-seq (transcription factor binding)?
2. Does FAVA improve the downstream functional association confidence scores (i.e., do edges identified via FAVA co-expression have higher precision in independent validation)?
3. How sensitive are results to VAE hyperparameters (latent dim, encoder depth, training epochs, likelihood model: Poisson vs. NB)?
4. Can FAVA be extended to multi-omics (combining scRNA-seq with protein levels, chromatin accessibility) to further denoise co-expression?
